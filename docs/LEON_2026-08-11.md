# Leon, Spain — the 2026-08-12 eclipse campaign data

**Date:** 2026-08-17, against v1.3.7
**Data:** `I:\FRA500 Leon Aug 2026`
**Status:** the calibration half is measured and built. The refraction half is scoped, with
the numbers that say how much it matters, and not yet done.

This is the first campaign whose calibration data went through
[`mee2024/calibration.py`](../mee2024/calibration.py) — F1 in [`ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md) —
and it is the dataset that feature was designed against. It is also the first dataset where
**vertical refraction, not distortion, is the dominant error term**, because the eclipse
happened about 11° above the horizon.

## 1. What was captured

| set | folders | frames | gain | exposure | altitude |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `DARKS` | 8 tiers | 50 each (one 49) | 0 and 101 | 0.1–2.0 s (G0), 4 s and 6 s (G101) | — |
| `FLATS` | 1 | 50 | 0 | 0.42979 s | ~88° (panel) |
| `Zenith 1`, `Zenith 2` | 6 fields each | 30 each | 101 | 4 s | ~80° |
| `Horizon 1/2/3` | 3 fields each | 45 each | 101 | 6 s | ~9.6° (airmass 5.8) |
| `Refraction mosaic` | 80 positions | 5 each | 101 | 6 s | 5.00° → 88.92° → 5.00° |
| `Eclipse/SCI_ladder` | 7 | mixed | 0 | ladder | ~11° |

Rig: **FRA500 + 0.7× reducer, ASI2600MM Pro**, cooled to a hard-coded **+10 °C setpoint**,
offset 50, true 16-bit (`ADCBITS = 16`, no `BITSHIFT`), 6248 × 4176 = 26.1 MP. Site
`OBSLAT 42.5799722`, `OBSLONG -5.57`, `LOCATION 'Leon'`.

**The dark tiers match the science exposures exactly** — G101/4 s ↔ Zenith, G101/6 s ↔
Horizon and mosaic, the G0 ladder ↔ the eclipse ladder. That is the whole reason the library
keys on gain and exposure and refuses to interpolate: every tier that is needed was captured.

The refraction mosaic is a **full meridian scan**: M01–M40 climb the southern sky at az 180
from 5.00° to 88.92° in ~2.15° steps, then M41–M80 descend the northern sky at az 000 back
to 5.00°. 400 frames. It is the dataset that can verify a refraction model against real star
positions rather than against a formula.

`Eclipse/CAL_piLeo` is empty and `SCI_ladder` still holds duplicate `(2).fits` files, as
expected — pre-processing selection is still in progress.

## 2. The calibration library, built


### 2.1 `BIASADU` is trustworthy on this camera — unlike the 533MM

Header 500.91 (G0) and 501.14 (G101) against measured 502.40 and 502.60: **within 0.3%**.
On the London ASI533MM the same keyword was out by a factor of 4.16 (393.59 claimed, 94.66
measured), which is why the builder checks rather than trusts. Here it checks out, and the
check is what lets us say so.

### 2.2 Dark current is not measurable at this temperature

The G0 median rises 502.396 → 502.438 across the whole 0.1–2.0 s ladder: **0.042 ADU total**,
or about 0.03 e⁻ at `EGAIN` 0.777. The last three tiers are identical to three decimals, so
the ladder has run into the resolution of the measurement rather than into a trend.

Read that as an upper bound, not a rate: **dark current at +10 °C is below what a 26 MP
median can resolve over two seconds.** It is consistent with the capture script's stated
0.003 e⁻/px/s. The practical consequence is the one the script already assumed — for these
exposures the darks are a **defect map and a bias**, not a signal to subtract.

### 2.3 Read noise measures lower than the header claims

| gain | measured σ | × `EGAIN` | header `RDNOISE` |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 3.35 ADU | 2.60 e⁻ | 3.49 e⁻ |
| 101 | 4.02 ADU | 1.05 e⁻ | 1.54 e⁻ |

Both about 25–30% below the header value, consistently across gains. `RDNOISE` is probably a
spec figure rather than a measurement of this body. **Worth resolving before it is used in an
error budget**, since a noise model that is 30% pessimistic makes every other term look
smaller than it is. The per-pixel σ maps are in the library beside each master if someone
wants to re-derive it.

### 2.4 The flat justifies taking no flat-darks — measured, not assumed

**Fill 50.7% of full scale** (33 223 of 65 535), which is where its capture script aimed
("histogram peaks mid-range, ~30–35k ADU") and inside the 25–75% band the no-flat-dark
argument needs. The unsubtracted offset pedestal is diluted by the signal, so at half scale
it leaves ~0.3% at a vignetted corner — smooth, multiplicative, centroid-irrelevant.

So for this campaign the decision to retire flat-darks is supported by the data rather than
by the argument alone. PRNU is 1.278%.

One flat set at gain 0 serves the gain-101 night data too, on the stated first-order
assumption that PRNU and vignetting do not depend on gain. The library applies it and
**says so** each time, rather than leaving the assumption implicit.

### 2.5 Why min-max rejection and not sigma-clipping

Worth recording, because sigma-clipping is the obvious first choice and it was tried first.

Clipping needs a spread to measure against, and the only spread a streaming pass has is the
per-pixel standard deviation — which the outlier itself inflates. One frame at 60 000 ADU
among forty-nine at 500 gives that pixel a σ near 17 800, so **even a 5σ cut keeps the
spike.** Verified as a test rather than reasoned about.

Dropping each pixel's own extreme high and low frame needs no scale estimate, comes free from
the same accumulators (`mean = (sum − min − max) / (n − 2)`), and discriminates on the right
axis: a cosmic ray is extreme in *one* frame, while a hot or telegraph pixel is high in
*every* frame and survives with 2 of 50 samples trimmed. It also halved the build time —
**582 s to 324 s** — because there is no second pass over 24 GB.

The σ map written beside each master is deliberately **untrimmed**: it is the only thing that
finds telegraph/RTS pixels, and trimming each pixel's extremes is exactly what would hide
them.

## 2.6 End to end, on the real data

Two of the six `Zenith 1` fields, chosen the way F10 now lets a user choose them, run through
the batch path with the library attached. This exercises the whole v1.3.7 chain at once on
26-megapixel frames.

| | `Z1_base` | `Z2_mid_left` |
|---|---|---|
| centroids | 8296 | 8080 |
| plate solved | yes (v2) | yes (v2) |
| stars in the fit | 732 | 694 |
| stage-2 rms | **115.9 mas** | **110.5 mas** |
| `nn_corr` | 0.183 | 0.155 |
| FWHM | 4.72″ | 4.69″ |
| PSF ellipticity | 0.111 | 0.112 |
| plate scale | 2.2060″/px | 2.2072″/px |
| dither span | 9.1 px | 10.9 px |
| epoch | 2026-08-11, **from the header** | 2026-08-11, from the header |
| dark applied | `dark_ASI2600MM_g101_4p000s_bin1` | same |
| flat applied | `flat_...bin1_f17000` (+ cross-gain note) | same |

Everything the release claims is visible in that one run: the right dark tier matched from the
frames' own gain and exposure, the flat applied with its assumption stated, the epoch read from
the header rather than guessed, the run written into `.../Zenith 1/` with the field tree inside
and `batch_summary.csv` at the root, a `field_summary.json` and a per-field `activity.jsonl`
inside each field folder, `TWOD_RESIDUALS.csv` in each stage-2 archive, and the stage-2 archive
name down to 49 characters.

**The plate scale supports F8.** Measured 2.2060 and 2.2072″/px against **2.2159″/px predicted
from `FOCALLEN` 350 and `XPIXSZ` 3.76** — agreement to **0.45%**. That is a usable prior for
collapsing the solver's FOV ladder, and the horizon fields at airmass 5.8 are where it would
earn its keep.

The dither span of 9–11 px is above the 3 px floor the dark-free hot-pixel search needs, so
these fields would have been reducible without darks at all — though with darks there is no
reason to.

**rms 111–116 mas against London's 68 mas** is not a regression: different rig, different
sampling. At 4.7″ FWHM on 2.21″ pixels the star images are ~2.1 px across, near the
undersampling limit, where centroid precision is worst. Refraction is not the cause — at 80°
altitude the curvature is 0.00 px (§4).

### 2.7 One warning had to be fixed to make this readable

The first end-to-end run printed *"the calibrated stack runs 66 ADU below zero... usually means
the darks do not match the lights"* — on a frame calibrated with the exactly-matching tier at
the same gain, exposure and setpoint.

The test was `if pedestal:`, i.e. *any* pixel below zero. On 26 million pixels with 4 ADU of
read noise the single lowest pixel sits five or six sigma under by construction, and dividing
by a vignetted flat deepens it, so that warning fired on **every correctly calibrated frame** —
the fastest way to teach someone to ignore a warning. It now reports the pedestal plainly
always, and warns only when more than 1% of the frame is negative, which is what a genuinely
mismatched dark looks like.

## 3. Environment, and why the defaults would have been wrong

`leon_temp_press_humid.csv` is UTF-16, tab separated, 4086 readings at 30 s from 18:45 UTC on
11 August to 04:48 UTC on 13 August. **The times in it are local (UTC+1)** — its own header
says `Original time zone UTC+1` — while the FITS headers are UTC.

The user's warning about excursions is correct and it matters: over the whole file pressure
ranges 893.0–928.7 hPa and temperature 20.1–38.7 °C, but **at the actual capture windows the
site was remarkably stable**:

| window (UTC) | n | temperature | humidity | pressure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Zenith 1, 11 Aug 22:16–22:31 | 46 | 24.5 °C | 32.6–33.6% | 895.4 hPa |
| DARKS, 11 Aug 22:34–22:47 | 28 | 24.4 °C | 32.4–32.6% | 895.5 hPa |
| FLATS, 11 Aug 23:05 | 6 | 24.3 °C | 32.4–32.8% | 895.4 hPa |
| Horizon 1, 11 Aug 23:16–23:31 | 32 | 24.2 °C | 32.4–32.7% | 895.3 hPa |
| **Eclipse, 12 Aug ~18:28** | 40 | **33.5 °C** | **20.7%** | **896.3 hPa** |
| Mosaic, 12 Aug 23:10–23:50 | 80 | 23.9 °C | 34.5–40.8% | 897.5 hPa |

So use the windowed values, never the file mean: the whole-file pressure mean of 907.6 hPa is
10 hPa off the site value, and the whole-file temperature mean of 27.3 °C splits the
difference between a 24 °C night and a 33.5 °C eclipse afternoon.

**The pipeline's defaults are `observation_pressure = 1010.0` and `observation_temp = 10.0`.**
On site it was 895–897 hPa at 24 °C, and 896 hPa at 33.5 °C on eclipse day. Refraction scales
as P/T, so the defaults overstate it by **15.5% at night and 18.1% on eclipse day**.

## 4. What that costs, and the part that is easy to overstate

Absolute refraction is mostly harmless: a uniform shift is absorbed by the plate solve, and a
uniform gradient across the field is absorbed by the fit's linear terms. What survives is the
**curvature** of refraction across the field — and near the horizon refraction is strongly
nonlinear in altitude, so over a 0.94° field it is not small.

The camera was set roughly horizon-parallel with the Sun low in the frame, so the gradient
runs along the short axis. At the measured site conditions, and with a plate scale of
2.216″/px from `FOCALLEN` 350 and `XPIXSZ` 3.76:

| altitude | dataset | total across field | **curvature the fit cannot absorb** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.16° | mosaic M01 | 66.6″ | **2.12″ (0.96 px)** |
| 9.62° | Horizon 1 | 25.2″ | **0.52″ (0.23 px)** |
| 11.6° | eclipse | 18.3″ | **0.32″ (0.15 px)** |
| 20.1° | mosaic M09 | 6.8″ | 0.07″ (0.03 px) |
| 45.9° | mosaic M20 | 1.6″ | 0.01″ |
| 79.9° | Zenith | 0.9″ | 0.00″ |

Against a stage-2 rms of ~68 mas, the 0.52″ at Horizon-1 altitude is **about 8× the noise
floor**, and a full pixel at the bottom of the mosaic. That confirms the report from the
field: at these altitudes vertical refraction is the dominant term.

**But the parameter error is the smaller half, and it would be easy to claim otherwise.**
Using 1010 hPa / 10 °C instead of the measured values changes the *curvature* by only 0.10″
at 9.62° and 0.39″ at 5.16°:

| altitude | measured curvature | with the defaults | error |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5.16° | 2.12″ | 2.51″ | +0.39″ (0.18 px) |
| 9.62° | 0.52″ | 0.62″ | +0.10″ (0.04 px) |
| 11.6° | 0.32″ | 0.38″ | +0.06″ (0.03 px) |

So there are two separate findings, and only the first is large:

1. **Refraction must be modelled at all.** `enable_corrections_ref` is `False` by default, and
   the app window cannot enable it — it is classic-UI-only. Not modelling it leaves 0.5–2″ of
   curvature in the residuals at horizon altitudes, or absorbs it into the fitted distortion
   coefficients, which contaminates the very thing the zenith fields exist to measure.
2. **Once it is modelled, feed it the real site.** Worth 0.1–0.4″ at these altitudes — small
   beside the first item, but still 1.5–6× the noise floor, and free: `OBSLAT`, `OBSLONG`,
   `SITEELEV`, `JD_UTC` and `FOCTEMP` are in every header, and pressure and humidity are in
   the CSV.

### 4.1 The zenith/horizon pairing is the right design

Falls out of the table above and is worth stating. At 80° the refraction curvature is 0.00 px,
so **the zenith fields measure distortion cleanly**. At 9.6° the curvature is 0.23 px, so the
horizon fields measure refraction — provided the distortion is held fixed at what the zenith
fields found.

The mechanism for that already exists: `distortion_fixed_coefficients` and
`distortion_reference_files`. Both are classic-UI-only, and both sit in Phase 2 of
[`UI_ROADMAP.md`](UI_ROADMAP.md). This campaign is the argument for promoting them.

The user reports the telescope was stable in rotation and plate scale across the 24 hours,
which is what makes transferring a distortion solution between the two nights legitimate;
polar alignment drifted from ~1′ to ~3′, which does not affect a per-field fit. Camera
rotation was ~3–4° off horizontal rather than 0°; that is a roll term the fit already solves
for, so it costs nothing beyond a slightly oblique refraction axis.

## 5. Open, and in order

1. **F7 — harvest the header and the CSV** into the correction inputs, and default the
   corrections on when the header supplies the site. This is now the top of
   [`ROADMAP.md`](ROADMAP.md) §6.
2. **Verify the refraction model against the mosaic.** 80 positions from 5° to 89° and back
   is a direct test of the formula against measured star positions — the thing the mosaic was
   captured for. Expect the model to be tested hardest at M01/M80.
3. **Fix the distortion from the zenith nights, then fit the horizon fields against it.**
   Needs `distortion_fixed_coefficients` reachable from something other than the classic UI.
4. **Resolve the `RDNOISE` discrepancy** (§2.3) before the noise model is used in anger.
5. **Re-run the calibration build when the eclipse frames are final**, since `CAL_piLeo` will
   add a tier and `SCI_ladder` currently holds duplicates.

## 6. Reproducing this

```bash
mee2024 calibrate --library LIB --darks "I:\FRA500 Leon Aug 2026\DARKS" --flats "I:\FRA500 Leon Aug 2026\FLATS"
```
```bash
mee2024 calibrate --library LIB --list
```
```bash
mee2024 calibrate --library LIB --match "I:\FRA500 Leon Aug 2026\Zenith\Zenith 1\Z1_base\22_16_15\*.fits"
```

The last one reports which masters *would* be applied to a field without running anything,
which is the quick way to check a library covers a night before starting fifty runs.
