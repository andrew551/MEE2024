# Portland zenith, 2026-07-29 — what the data says

**Dataset:** `Portland zenith.zip`, six zenith fields reduced under v1.3.6.
**Date of this note:** 2026-08-10. **Purpose:** a dress rehearsal for Leon, 2026-08-12.

Everything below was measured from the run's own output — `batch_summary.json`,
`activity.jsonl`, and the twelve archives. No raw frames were available, and that limits
two of the questions; where it does, it says so.

---

## 0. The run in one paragraph

Six fields, thirty frames each, captured between 02:09:54 and 02:22:53 with 153–165 s
between field starts. A 2×3 grid: header pointings at RA 307.68° / 313.92° and Dec
42.07° / 45.07° / 48.07°, so the fields do **not** overlap (grid step 4.4° × 3.0° against a
3.83° × 2.56° field of view). Sensor 6248 × 4176 at 2.2098″/px, which is an IMX571 body at
about 351 mm of focal length. All six plate-solved on the first attempt. Global rms
195–240 mas.

---

## 1. Did the software do what it should?

**Yes, and the fixes from last week are visible in the output.**

- **The header date was used.** Every field carries `observation_date 2026-07-29`,
  `date_guessed? false`, `date_guess_error_days null`. This is I0 working in folder mode —
  the London set of 2026-08-06 guessed sixteen different epochs spanning six months
  (ROADMAP §1.3).
- **Provenance is there.** `effective_options` (81 keys), `calibration`, `source_folder`,
  `alignment.shifts_px`, and the solve block are all in `results.txt` — I3, I6 and I16.
  The per-frame shifts in particular are what §3 below is built from; a week ago they
  went to a `print()`.
- **The solves were easy.** `n_clusters_checked: 1`, `anchor_rounds_used: 1`,
  `noise_px_used: 0.3` (the default, not escalated), 97–100+ stars matched against a
  threshold of 14. Nothing like the marginal ladder of ROADMAP §1.9.
- **The dark-free hot-pixel search had what it needs.** No darks or flats were taken, so
  it fell back to the dither — and the dither here is 55–68 px, far above the 3 px floor.
  It found 37–45 hot pixels per field.

Three things worth flagging, none of them failures:

- **Only one pattern-database layer is installed** (`patdb_g13_t17k`) on the reducing
  machine — the same single-layer state that cost two fields in the London set. It was
  not tested here, because nothing was marginal. The pre-flight check in ROADMAP §1.9
  still wants doing before the eclipse.
- **The double-star search was capped at G<15 by the catalogue** in all six fields, having
  asked for 17.0. `remove_double_tab2` is on, so unflagged close companions stayed in the
  fit. On a 2.2″/px scale with a 5.9″ FWHM a blend is an astrometric error, not a
  detection.
- **`enable_corrections` is False.** It costs nothing here — see §7 — but it will not be
  optional at Leon.

## 2. The fit: 195–240 mas, and what limits it

| field | rms (mas) | stars | nn_corr | centroids |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1_base | 195.0 | 2707 | +0.121 | 7375 |
| Z2_mid_left | 222.1 | 4235 | +0.054 | 10827 |
| Z3_top_left | 217.1 | 3646 | +0.096 | 10201 |
| Z4_top_right | 237.9 | 3537 | +0.024 | 9828 |
| Z5_mid_right | 240.1 | 6075 | +0.012 | 12604 |
| Z6_bottom_right | 226.4 | 2392 | +0.060 | 6284 |

Against the London set's 67.7 mas this is three times worse, and it is worth knowing why
before drawing conclusions from eclipse data taken the same way.

**Plate scale is excellent:** 2.209832″/px with a scatter of **5 ppm** across the six
fields — better than London's 7 ppm over sixteen. Focus and optical train were stable
across the thirteen minutes.

**The residual grows towards the field edge, by a factor of three to four.** Median
|residual| in mas, by fraction of the half-diagonal:

| | 15% | 30% | 45% | 60% | 75% | 90% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| V < 10.5 | 80 | 93 | 147 | 185 | 230 | 343 |
| V > 10.5 | 64 | 65 | 88 | 105 | 132 | 183 |

(Z1; the other five are the same shape.) Two things follow. First, **bright stars are
worse than faint ones at every radius** — so this is not photon noise, which would run the
other way, and the faint-end floor of 81–118 mas at V = 11–12 is about where a
photon-limited estimate from the detection SNR lands. Second, the growth is **radial**:
splitting the residual into radial and tangential components gives 254 vs 153 mas in the
outer band against 79 vs 81 in the inner one.

**It is not the distortion order.** Refitting Z1's own centroids at three orders:

| order | rms | nn_corr | 0–35% | 35–60% | 60–80% | 80–100% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| cubic | 195.0 | +0.121 | 70 | 101 | 133 | 202 |
| quintic | 188.3 | +0.044 | 67 | 94 | 125 | 185 |
| septic | 186.8 | +0.036 | 67 | 94 | 121 | 198 |

Quintic is worth having — `nn_corr` falls from 0.121 to 0.044, which says the cubic really
was leaving correlated structure — but it buys 3% of rms and does nothing to the edge. The
radial mean residual is within ±18 mas of zero in every band at every order, so there is no
under-fitted radial term left to find. **What grows at the edge is the star images
themselves** (§5), and no polynomial in position can fix a PSF that varies with position.

## 3. Polar alignment: off by degrees, and in azimuth

Every frame's alignment shift is now on disk, so this is measured rather than inferred.
Mapping the shifts through a pixel→sky Jacobian fitted from each field's own matched-star
table (which sidesteps every roll and mirror convention; the recovered scale agrees with
the solver's to 0.1% and the map is orthogonal to 2×10⁻³):

| field | dRA·cosδ | dDec | (arcsec per frame) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Z1_base | +0.039 | −4.281 | |
| Z2_mid_left | +0.827 | −4.154 | |
| Z3_top_left | +1.939 | −4.493 | |
| Z4_top_right | +0.797 | −4.348 | |
| Z5_mid_right | +1.480 | −4.403 | |
| Z6_bottom_right | −0.075 | −4.388 | |

**97% of the drift is in declination.** A tracking-rate error produces declination drift of
exactly zero, so this is a polar-axis error and not a rate error — that alternative is
excluded, not merely disfavoured.

The frame cadence is not in the data (`read_observation_date` reads frame 0 only — F5/F7),
but it is bounded: thirty frames plus a slew fit between consecutive folder timestamps, the
shortest gap being 153 s, so the cadence is **at most 5.28 s**. That makes the drift **at
least 51″/min**, and

| cadence | polar error |
|---|---|
| 5.28 s (the hard bound) | 194′ = 3.2° |
| 5.0 s | 205′ = 3.4° |
| 4.5 s | 227′ = 3.8° |
| 4.0 s | 256′ = 4.3° |

using ω·ε = 15.041″/s × ε, the same relation that gave London its 0.5′ (ROADMAP §1.4).
So: **three to four degrees**, and no plausible cadence brings it below 3.2°.

**It is an azimuth error, not an altitude one.** The fields sit at hour angles of +24° to
+29°, near the meridian, and declination drift near the meridian is the textbook signature
of azimuth error; altitude error shows itself in declination only well away from it.
Solving for the pole-offset direction puts it within ~11° of the pure-azimuth axis, so the
altitude setting is good to well under a degree while the bearing is out by three or four.
That is what aiming a mount by compass or by eye produces — the magnetic declination at
Portland, Oregon is about 15° east. (Which way round in azimuth depends on a sign
convention this dataset cannot settle; the magnitude does not.)

For scale: this is 400× the London mount's 0.5′, and it means the polar term now dominates
everything the mount does.

## 4. Periodic error: present, and ordinary

The drift is very nearly a straight line. Its departure from one, per field, after removing
the two discrete jumps found in §4.1:

| field | rms | peak-to-peak |
|---|---|---|
| Z1_base | 0.53″ | 2.40″ |
| Z2_mid_left | 0.61″ | 2.27″ |
| Z3_top_left | 0.67″ | 2.70″ |
| Z4_top_right | 0.75″ | 2.31″ |
| Z5_mid_right | 0.92″ | 3.03″ |
| Z6_bottom_right | 1.00″ | 3.65″ |

And the drift *rate* varies between fields: 4.281, 4.239, 4.756, 4.423, 4.653, 4.546 arcsec
per frame — **4.6% scatter** about the mean, over a thirteen-minute window that spans
one to two worm cycles.

Two independent readings of the same thing. The rate scatter implies a rate modulation of
0.055–0.065″/s, which for a worm period in the HEQ5/EQ6 range of 480–640 s is an amplitude
of **±4 to ±7 arcsec**. The within-field curvature says the same: a ±5″ sinusoid at 540 s,
sampled over thirty frames at 4.5 s, leaves 0.43″ rms about a straight line, against the
0.53–1.00″ observed.

**So yes, there is periodic error, at ±4–7″ — statistically the same mount behaviour as
London's ±7.7″.** The difference is that here it is 5% of the motion instead of nearly all
of it. Six rate samples over 1.5 cycles cannot pin the period; that would need per-frame
timestamps (F5) and a longer run.

### 4.1 Two discrete jumps, which are not periodic error

Robust outlier detection on the per-frame steps finds:

- **Z3, frame 28: a 7.59 px step** against a 2.14 px median (a 5.5 px excess, 30 MAD).
  This single event is why an earlier pass showed Z3 as four times less straight than the
  others; excluded, it is unremarkable. 7.6 px is 17 arcsec in one frame interval.
- **Z6, frame 12: 3.02 px** against 2.05 px median. Marginal, but the same shape.

A gust, a cable tug, or a slip. Worth knowing that they happen roughly once per 90 frames
on this setup, because during totality there is no second chance at the frame.

## 5. Focus: correctly focused, but the field is soft off axis

Per-star FWHM from the 1800+ stars measured in each field:

| | median | inner 35% | outer 25% | ratio | tilt in x | tilt in y | radial |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | 3.298 | 2.888 | 4.089 | 1.42 | −0.021 | −0.299 | +1.957 |
| Z2 | 3.310 | 2.914 | 4.112 | 1.41 | +0.002 | −0.303 | +1.911 |
| Z3 | 3.363 | 2.975 | 4.112 | 1.38 | +0.001 | −0.271 | +1.872 |
| Z4 | 3.432 | 2.958 | 4.169 | 1.41 | −0.008 | −0.290 | +1.911 |
| Z5 | 3.329 | 2.964 | 4.103 | 1.38 | +0.021 | −0.297 | +1.950 |
| Z6 | 3.470 | 2.984 | 4.226 | 1.42 | −0.023 | −0.298 | +2.012 |

(pixels; the tilt and radial columns are the fitted coefficients across the half-width,
half-height and half-diagonal respectively.)

Three readings, and they agree across all six fields to within a few percent:

1. **The focus itself is right.** The stacked PSF is cuspy — the azimuthal profile peaks at
   r = 0 and falls monotonically, 0.096 → 0.061 → 0.040 → 0.020 over the first two pixels.
   A defocused star from an unobstructed lens is flat-topped; this is the opposite shape.
   Half-power radius 1.25 px, so FWHM 2.50 px = 5.5″.
2. **The sensor is square to the optical axis.** The x-tilt coefficient is ±0.02 px, which
   is nothing; the y-tilt is −0.29 px across a half-height, small beside the radial term.
3. **The field is not flat.** The radial term is +1.9 to +2.0 px, and the corner is
   **1.4× the centre**. That is field curvature or an unmatched flattener spacing, not
   focus, and on a 43 mm-diagonal sensor at 351 mm it is exactly where one would expect to
   find it. This is what §2 is measuring at the field edge.

**There is a lot of light outside the core.** 50% of the flux is inside r = 2.6″, but 80%
needs 6.0″ and 90% needs 8.6″. At r = 7 px the profile is 2.7×10⁻³ of the peak where a
Gaussian of the same FWHM would be 4×10⁻¹⁰. Those wings, and the fact that they grow and
turn asymmetric off axis, are the most likely reason bright stars centroid *worse* than
faint ones: the brighter the star, the further into the asymmetric wings the centroid
window reaches.

**Trailing is not the main problem.** The coherent (vector-mean) ellipticity across the
frame is 0.7–3.6%, aligned within 20–25° of the drift direction in the five fields where it
is measurable at all — so it is trailing, and it is small. The per-star median ellipticity
quoted in the summary (0.066–0.147) is a positive-definite quantity and is inflated by
noise; the vector mean is the honest one.

**Actionable:** check the backfocus spacing. Recovering the corner to the centre's 2.9 px
would take the outer-band residual from ~200 mas towards ~100 and the global rms towards
140. That is a bigger win than anything in the software.

## 6. Settling: sufficient, with room to spare

Comparing the first inter-frame step after each slew with the steady-state median:

| field | first step | steady | excess |
|---|---|---|---|
| Z1_base | 1.742 | 1.970 ± 0.109 | −0.229 px (−2.1σ) |
| Z2_mid_left | 2.049 | 1.953 ± 0.149 | +0.096 px (+0.6σ) |
| Z3_top_left | 1.960 | 2.139 ± 1.079 | −0.179 px (−0.2σ) |
| Z4_top_right | 1.811 | 2.018 ± 0.200 | −0.207 px (−1.0σ) |
| Z5_mid_right | 2.178 | 2.115 ± 0.153 | +0.063 px (+0.4σ) |
| Z6_bottom_right | 2.221 | 2.014 ± 0.270 | +0.207 px (+0.8σ) |

Mean excess −0.008 px. Three of six are *negative* — the first step is smaller than the
steady rate, which is the opposite of a settling transient. The across-track component of
the first step is inside its own scatter in every field.

**The mount was settled by the first frame.** The data would have shown a transient above
about 0.2–0.3 px (0.5–0.7″); there is none. Same conclusion as the London set, and the same
consequence: dithering between exposures is mechanically practical here, and would cost
nothing.

## 7. Noise

The honest answer is that **this cannot be measured from the reduced output**, and the
reason is worth recording: no darks or flats were taken (`n_darks: 0, n_flats: 0`), and the
run does not harvest `EGAIN`, `RDNOISE`, `GAIN`, `OFFSET` or `CCD-TEMP` from the FITS
headers (F7 is unimplemented). So read noise, dark current and sensor temperature are all
absent from the record. For the London set those numbers came from fifty darks and fifty
flats, and they are how §1.8 of the roadmap exists at all.

What the data does support:

- The centroid residual bottoms out at **81–118 mas at V = 11–12** and rises at both ends.
  The faint side is about where a photon-limited estimate from the detection SNR lands, so
  the faint end behaves like a noise-limited measurement should.
- The bright end sits **five to ten times above** that floor, which is a systematic, and
  §5 says what kind.
- The dark-free defect search found only 37–45 hot pixels per field out of 19 000–37 000
  bright candidates. The London ASI533 darks found 13 290 defects above the 20σ cut. The
  dark-free path catches the worst offenders and nothing else — which is fine when the
  dither is 55–68 px and every defect lands on a different star each frame, and not fine at
  all on a stack that does not move.

**Take darks at Leon**, at every exposure of the ladder. Defect amplitude is
`pedestal + rate × t`, so tiers cannot be extrapolated from one another (ROADMAP §1.8), and
fifty frames at each of five exposures is about four minutes.

## 8. What this means for Leon, 2026-08-12

At Leon (42.60°N, 5.57°W) the Sun is at roughly **16–18° altitude** during totality, around
17:45–18:00 UT. Consequences, in order of how much they matter:

1. **Fix the polar alignment.** Not because of trailing — at ladder exposures the trailing
   from a 3.5° error is 0.04 px at 0.1 s and 0.9 px at 2.4 s, and only the longest rung
   suffers (2.7% broadening) — but because of two other things:
   - **The field walks 153″ = 69 px per 180 s.** Alignment removes it from the stack; it
     does not remove it from the *framing*, and the corona will not stay where it was put.
   - **Field rotation.** The same misalignment carries a residual rotation of up to
     0.85″/s, complementary to the drift and depending on where the mount points — at Leon,
     with the Sun in the west at low altitude, the split will be different from these
     zenith fields and may favour rotation. Up to 153″ over 180 s is **2.8 px of smear at
     the frame corner that a translation-only alignment cannot remove**. This makes F2
     (per-frame affine alignment) matter far more here than it did in London, where the
     whole term was 0.08 px.

   A twenty-minute SharpCap polar alignment takes 3.5° to under 1′ and removes all of this.

2. **Turn the refraction correction on, and give it a site and a time.** At 17° altitude
   the differential refraction across a 2.56° field is about 25″ of linear compression and
   1″ of quadratic. The linear part disappears into the plate scale and the quadratic into
   the distortion polynomial, so the *rms will not show it* — but the distortion solution
   will be wrong by an amount that is degenerate with the deflection signal. This needs
   `enable_corrections_ref`, `observation_lat/long` and `observation_time`, none of which
   the app window can set today (ROADMAP F9, Q1). **This is a blocker, not a
   convenience.**

3. **Check the backfocus.** §5: the corner is 1.4× the centre, and it costs more rms than
   anything in the software.

4. **Take darks at every ladder exposure**, and flats. §7.

5. **Use quintic, not cubic.** Free, and `nn_corr` says the cubic is leaving structure.

6. **Build the full pattern-database layer set on the reducing machine** before the day,
   and confirm `layers_used` shows more than one.

7. **Dither.** The mount settles inside one frame (§6), and the drift already supplies
   55–68 px of accidental dither here — but a properly aligned mount will not, and 20–50 px
   of deliberate dither costs 1–2% of the field and no trailing at all.

## 9. Method notes

- The pixel→sky map was fitted from each field's own `CATALOGUE_MATCHED_ERRORS.csv` rather
  than reconstructed from `roll` and `mirror`. It is checked two ways: the recovered scale
  matches the solver's to 0.1%, and the map is orthogonal to 2×10⁻³.
- `shifts_px` is `(position in frame 0 − position in frame i)` in `(row, col)`, so the
  image moves by *minus* the recorded shift. The magnitude conclusions do not depend on
  this; the drift direction does.
- The azimuth-versus-altitude split is asserted from the classical drift-alignment rule
  (declination drift near the meridian is azimuth error) as well as from the fitted
  pole-offset direction, because the two do not share a sign convention and agreeing is
  worth more than either alone.
- The polar error and the periodic-error amplitude both scale with the frame cadence, which
  is not recorded. Every cadence-dependent number above is quoted against the hard upper
  bound (5.28 s, from the folder timestamps) and against plausible values. **F5 and F7
  would remove this whole caveat** — `JD_UTC` is in every frame header and the pipeline
  reads only frame 0's.
- Totality timing at Leon is from the geocentric Sun–Moon separation, not topocentric
  circumstances, so it may be a few minutes out. The Sun's altitude changes about 1° per
  five minutes, so the conclusions in §8 are unaffected.
