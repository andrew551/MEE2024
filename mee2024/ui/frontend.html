Distortion field

          

            How far the optics displace a star from where an ideal projection would put
            it. Arrows show direction and size; the map shows magnitude.
          

          

        

        

          

            
Advanced analysis

            

            
          

          

            The fitted displacement as a surface with every measured star on it, and a map
            of where residuals stay correlated across the detector. For diagnosing the fit
            rather than reading off the result.
          

          

            

              
              
              
              
            

            

              
              

                Residual exaggeration
                
                

                
              

              

                Drag to rotate, scroll to zoom, double-click to reset. Each point is one
                star, sitting off the surface by its residual. Even peppering means the
                order is sufficient — a carpet undulating coherently above and below
                means it is not. On a good fit the residuals are far smaller than the
                distortion itself, so raise the exaggeration to see their structure.
              

              

            

            

              
              

                Bins per axis
                
                12
              

              

                Each cell averages, over the stars inside it, the cosine of the angle
                between a star's residual and its nearest neighbour's. Near zero is
                uncorrelated noise. A warm patch is a region where residuals point the same
                way — a local optical imperfection the global polynomial has not
                absorbed. The single figure in the score cards is this map averaged.
              

              

            

          

          

        


        

          

            
Point spread function

            

            
          

          

            The shape of a star on the detector: how sharp, how round, and whether one
            answer holds across the whole field. For judging the optics and the night
            rather than the analysis.
          

          

            

            
            

              The average star, brightest-first, on a logarithmic scale. Ground-based star
              images carry wings a Gaussian lacks; the Moffat curve models them, and its
              β says whose wings they are (≈4.8 is pure atmosphere, lower means
              the optics contribute).
            

            
            
            

              Star size across the field, with sticks showing the direction stars are
              stretched. Uniform colour and short sticks mean one PSF describes the whole
              frame; size growing toward a corner is focal-plane tilt, sticks pointing at
              a common centre is coma from decentred optics.
